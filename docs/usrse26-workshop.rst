USRSE'26 Interactive Workshop
=============================

A Group Attempt at Threat Modeling for Research Software Systems
----------------------------------------------------------------

**October 19–21, 2026 · San Jose, California**

Research software operates within complex, distributed environments shaped by
dependencies, infrastructure, people, and institutional practices. These
conditions make security risks difficult to define and conventional threat
modeling difficult to apply.

This interactive USRSE'26 workshop brings research software engineers together
to develop a more concrete, shared understanding of that threat surface.
Participants will:

- examine a high-level system model for a typical research software project
- critique and specialize an initial draft threat model for their own contexts
- identify threats involving software supply chains, dependency management,
  and organizational factors
- translate broad security concerns into defined threats and practical
  hardening measures

How the Workshop Supports CROSS
-------------------------------

CROSS—the **Community aROund Securing the Research Software Supply Chain**—is
an NSF-supported planning effort that brings together researchers, research
software engineers, and government stakeholders to identify risks and develop
a roadmap for strengthening research software supply chains.

Community workshops are central to that goal. The USRSE'26 session creates a
structured way for practitioners to test the project's early system and threat
models against real research software environments. Participants' experience
will help CROSS:

- validate how research software supply chains are represented
- reveal technical, human, and organizational threats that an abstract model
  may overlook
- understand how risks and feasible mitigations vary across projects and
  institutions
- refine a shared system model, threat model, and future security roadmap

In this way, the workshop is both a practical threat-modeling exercise for
attendees and a community input mechanism for the broader CROSS effort.
